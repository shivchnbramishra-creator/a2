The pantry and grill guide

Essential Japanese-inspired pantry ingredients, how to cook perfect rice, how to manage a charcoal grill and the knife skills that make prep quick and calm.

Rice

Cooking perfect short-grain rice

Short-grain rice is slightly sticky and tender, ideal for bowls and rice balls. Rinsing removes excess surface starch for a clean texture.

  1. Rinse the rice in cold water, swirling gently, until the water runs almost clear.
  2. Drain well, then add water: roughly 1.1–1.2 parts water to 1 part rice by volume.
  3. Soak for 20–30 minutes if you have time.
  4. Bring to the boil, cover tightly and simmer on the lowest heat for 12–15 minutes.
  5. Rest, covered and off the heat, for 10 minutes before fluffing.

Cool leftover rice quickly and refrigerate within an hour; eat within a day and reheat until steaming hot.

Charcoal

Lighting and managing a charcoal grill

  • Use a chimney starter with newspaper or natural firelighters rather than lighter fluid, which can taint flavour.
  • Wait until the coals glow red and are covered with grey ash, usually 15–20 minutes.
  • Create two zones: coals piled on one side for searing, and a cooler side for gentle cooking.
  • Use the vents to control heat: more air means hotter coals, less air cools them.
  • Always grill outdoors in a well-ventilated area, keep water nearby and never leave a lit grill unattended.

Hand test

Hold your palm about 12 cm above the grate. Two seconds is high heat, four to five seconds is medium and over seven is low.

Skewer smarts

Soak bamboo skewers for 30 minutes so they don’t burn, and use two parallel skewers to stop pieces spinning.

Safe finish

Let coals burn out completely and cool fully before disposing of ash in a metal container.

Knife skills

Simple cuts for faster prep

  • Matchsticks: thin strips of carrot, cucumber and daikon for bowls and salads.
  • Diagonal slices: spring onions and courgettes cut on an angle cook evenly and look elegant.
  • Fine mince: ginger and garlic for sauces, or use a fine grater instead.

A sharp knife is a safer knife: it needs less pressure and is less likely to slip.

Umami

Building savoury flavour in plant-based cooking

Mushrooms, seaweed, miso, soy and ripe tomatoes are rich in natural umami. Combining two or more creates a deeper, rounder savouriness than any one alone.

Try a simple stock of kombu and dried shiitake, soaked in cold water overnight, as the base for soups, noodle broths and cooking rice.

Quick pickles

Ten-minute pickles for every bowl

Thinly slice cucumber, radish or carrot, toss with a pinch of salt and leave for five minutes. Squeeze out the liquid, then add a splash of rice vinegar, a little sugar and some julienned ginger.

Keep in a clean jar in the fridge and use within three days. Their bright acidity balances rich, savoury dishes perfectly.
